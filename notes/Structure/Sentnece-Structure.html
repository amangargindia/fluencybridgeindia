2. Concept Intro (Shart Wale Vakya)

Conditional sentences ka matlab hai 'Shart' (Condition). Inme do hisse (clauses) hote hain:

  • Condition (If Clause): Jo shart rakhta hai. "Agar tum mehnat karoge..."
  • Result (Main Clause): Us shart ka natija. "...toh tum pass ho jaoge."

3. Placement: 'If' in Front vs Middle

Aap sentence ko do tareeko se likh sakte hain. Meaning bilkul same rehta hai, bas punctuation (comma) ka fark aata hai.

Pattern 1: If at the Front

Formula: If + Condition , Result

Rule: Jab 'If' sentence ke shuru mein aaye, toh dono hisso ke beech comma (,) lagana zaroori hai.

Example: If it rains, we will stay home.

(Agar barish hui, hum ghar par rahenge.)

Pattern 2: If in the Middle

Formula: Result + if + Condition

Rule: Jab 'If' beech mein aaye, toh koi comma (,) NAHI lagta kyunki 'if' khud ek bridge/connector ka kaam kar raha hai.

Example: We will stay home if it rains.

(Hum ghar rahenge agar barish hui.)

4. Implied Alternatives (Chhupe hue meaning)

Jab hum ek condition batate hain, toh hum indirectly uska opposite bhi bol rahe hote hain.

Sentence: "If you pay the bill, the electricity won't be cut."

Implied (Chhupa hua matlab): Agar tumne bill nahi bhara, toh light kat jayegi (If you don't pay, it will be cut). Conditionals hamesha ek "alternative timeline" present karte hain.

5. Top Mistakes (Aksar hone wali galtiyan)

  • Mistake 1: 'If' wale hisse mein 'will' lagana.

    Wrong: If it will rain, I will not go.

    Right: If it rains, I will not go.

    Logic: Condition (shart) present tense mein likhi jati hai (rains), aur natija future mein (will not go).

  • Mistake 2: Beech mein 'If' hone par comma lagana.

    Wrong: I will help you , if you ask.

    Right: I will help you if you ask.

The Mumbai Monsoon Dilemma

Rohan was planning a trip to Marine Drive with his friends. The only problem was the unpredictable Mumbai weather.

"If it rains heavily, the trains will stop working," Rohan thought to himself. He checked his phone. The weather app showed dark clouds.

His friend Aman called him. "Are we still going?" Aman asked.

Rohan replied, "We will go if the weather clears up by 4 PM. However, if it starts pouring, we will just meet at my house."

Aman agreed. "Okay, I will bring some snacks if we meet at your place. But remember, my bike has a flat tire. I will have to take an auto if I come to your house."

"No problem," Rohan said. "If you can't find an auto, I will come and pick you up. Just let me know."

Through this short conversation, they had completely planned for two different realities, entirely depending on the word 'if'.
