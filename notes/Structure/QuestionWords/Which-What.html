01. Prerequisites (Pehle ye samjho)

Before mastering Which and What, you should have a basic idea of:

  • Interrogative Pronouns (Click to search) - Words used to ask questions.
  • Determiners (Click to search) - Words placed in front of a noun to make it clear what the noun refers to.

02. The Core Concept (The Secret Rule)

Ek simple rule yaad rakho: Choices ka limit.

WHEN TO USE 'WHICH'

Jab aapke paas LIMITED CHOICES (ginti ke options) hote hain. Aapko pata hai ki options kya hain, aur unme se ek chunna hai.

"Which shirt should I buy? The red one or the blue one?" (Yahan sirf 2 option hain)

WHEN TO USE 'WHAT'

Jab aapke paas UNLIMITED CHOICES (bahut saare options) hote hain ya options open-ended hote hain.

"What is your favourite colour?" (Duniya mein hazaaron colours hain, koi limit nahi hai)

03. Deep Dive & Logical Examples

  • Scenario A: Asking about someone's name Wrong: Which is your name? Right: What is your name? Logic: Duniya mein lakho names hain. Koi limited menu nahi hai names ka jisme se ek choose karna ho. Isliye 'What' aayega.
  • Scenario B: Choosing a train to Delhi Wrong: What train are you taking to Delhi? Shatabdi or Rajdhani? Right: Which train are you taking to Delhi? Shatabdi or Rajdhani? Logic: Yahan specifically 2 trains ke beech choice di gayi hai (Shatabdi ya Rajdhani). Choices limited hain, so 'Which' is the correct answer.
  • Scenario C: The tricky implied limits

    "Which hand do you write with?" (Right ✅)

    Logic: Insaan ke paas naturally 2 hi haath hote hain (Left or Right). Options automatically limited hain, chahe sentence mein bola na gaya ho. Isliye 'What hand' bolna galat hai.

04. Top Mistakes People Make (Dhyan rakhna!)

Mistake 1: Using 'What' for fixed menus.

Restaurant mein menu dekh kar: What dish should we order from here? -> Which dish should we order? (Kyunki menu limited hota hai).

Mistake 2: Using 'Which' for general knowledge.

Gk puchte waqt: Which is the capital of India? -> What is the capital of India? (Though there is one right answer, the scope of the question before knowing the answer is wide open).

*Note: If you show a map with 4 cities and ask, then "Which of these is the capital?" becomes correct.

Story Time: A Trip to the Market

Rahul and Anjali were standing outside the busy Sarojini Market in Delhi. Rahul looked at the endless shops and asked, "What do you want to buy today?"

Anjali thought for a moment. There were so many options. "I need some clothes for college," she replied. As they walked in, they saw two large stalls selling jeans. Rahul pointed and asked, "Which stall should we go to? The left one or the right one?"

"Let's go to the right one," Anjali decided. Inside, the shopkeeper showed her a pile of jeans. She picked up a blue pair and a black pair. The shopkeeper smiled and asked, "Which color do you prefer, Madam?"

"The black one looks better," she said. After buying the jeans, Rahul realized he was hungry. He looked at the vast food court menu. "What should we eat?" he wondered aloud.

Anjali knew Rahul loved either Momos or Chole Bhature. "Which of your favorites do you want right now? Momos or Bhature?" she asked playfully.

Rahul laughed. "Momos! By the way, what time is it?"

"It's 6 PM," Anjali replied. "Let's eat and head back home."

Story Breakdown: Notice how What was used for open questions ("What do you want to buy?", "What should we eat?", "What time is it?"). And Which was used when choices were presented ("Which stall... left or right?", "Which color...", "Which of your favorites...").
