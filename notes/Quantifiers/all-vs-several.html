All vs Several: Nuances & Mind Map

Category: Quantifiers
Prerequisite: Countable and Uncountable Nouns

  • All: Means 100% of the quantity. Can be used with both countable ("All students") and uncountable nouns ("All water").
  • Several: Means more than two but not many (usually 3 to 7). Only used with plural countable nouns ("Several days").

Logical Example: "I ate all the apples" means none are left. "I ate several apples" means I ate a few, but there might be more.

Top Mistakes

  • Using 'several' with uncountable nouns (Wrong: "I drank several water").
  • Forgetting 'of the' when a pronoun follows 'all' (Wrong: "All them went" -> Right: "All of them went").

Story Context

Rohan was organizing a wedding. He invited all his relatives. However, only several cousins arrived early to help. He told them, "I need all the chairs arranged." Several hours passed, and finally, all the preparations were complete.
