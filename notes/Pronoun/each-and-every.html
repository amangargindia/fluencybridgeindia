Each vs Every: The Breakdown

Category: Pronoun / Determiner
Prerequisite: Singular vs Plural rules

  • Each: Used for 2 or more items. Focuses on individual members of a group (individuality).
  • Every: Used for 3 or more items. Focuses on the group as a whole, similar to 'all', but grammatically singular.
  • Rule: Both are always followed by singular countable nouns and singular verbs! ("Every student is" not "Every students are").

Why it matters: "Each of the two boys" is correct. "Every of the two boys" is wrong because 'every' cannot be used for just two.

Top Mistakes

  • Using plural verbs with them (Wrong: "Every person have a car" -> Right: "Every person has a car").
  • Using 'every' for only two things (Wrong: "He had a ring on every hand" -> Right: "He had a ring on each hand").

Story Context

Every morning, the teacher greets her class. She looks at each student individually. Every student wears a uniform, but each uniform has a slightly different name tag. The teacher ensures each of the two doors is locked before starting.
