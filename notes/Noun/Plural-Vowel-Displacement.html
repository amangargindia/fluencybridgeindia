2. History & Logic (I-Mutation)

English grammer mein "Foot" ka plural "Feet" kyun hota hai, "Foots" kyun nahi? Iska karan Old English ke time ki pronunciation hai. Ise linguists I-mutation ya I-umlaut kehte hain.

  • Step 1: The Old Suffix Hazaron saal pehle, Germanic languages (jinse English aayi) plural banane ke liye word ke end mein -i ya -iz lagate the. Jaise 'fōt' (foot) ka plural tha 'fōti'.
  • Step 2: Lazy Pronunciation (Assimilation) Jab log 'fōti' bolte the, 'o' (jo piche se bolte hain) ke baad turant 'i' (jo aage se bolte hain) bolna mushkil hota tha. Muh ko jaldi aage laane ke chakkar mein, beecho-beech ka vowel badal gaya. 'o' dheere-dheere 'e' ban gaya.
  • Step 3: The Suffix Drops Waqt ke sath, end ka '-i' sound bilkul drop ho gaya, aur sirf badla hua beech ka vowel reh gaya.
    Result: fōt ➔ fōti ➔ fēt ➔ Feet.

4. Top Mistakes

Mistake 1: Adding 's' to mutated plurals

Log bolte hain "mens" ya "womens".
Wrong: "I saw many womens."
Right: "I saw many women."
Note: "Women's" with an apostrophe is possessive (auraton ka), not plural.

Mistake 2: Using 'a/an' with plurals

Kyunki word mein 's' nahi laga hota, log bhool jate hain ki ye plural hai.
Wrong: "He is a men."
Right: "He is a man." OR "They are men."

Mistake 3: Over-applying the rule

Har 'oo' wala word 'ee' nahi banta. Jaise Book ka plural Beak nahi hota.
Right: Book -> Books, Boot -> Boots.

A Day in the Village

In a quiet little village, an old man named Arthur lived near a large pond. He had a peculiar habit; every morning, he would walk barefoot, letting the wet grass tickle his feet. He believed it was good for his health, though other men in the village thought it was just silly.

Arthur had a beautiful flock of birds. Usually, a single goose would guard the yard, but by the water, dozens of geese would gather and honk loudly at anyone passing by.

One afternoon, a woman from the neighborhood came running to Arthur. She was terrified. "There is a mouse in my kitchen!" she cried. Arthur chuckled, knowing that where there is one, there are usually several mice hiding in the walls.

He grabbed a wooden trap and gave her a wide smile, showing off his perfectly white teeth. Surprisingly, he had never lost a single tooth despite his old age. As he walked toward her house, he noticed a group of women chatting outside. They were complaining about the schools checking the children's hair for lice, making sure no child brought even a single louse back home.

Arthur solved the kitchen problem quickly. He washed his foot before entering his own home, content with another simple day.
