1. Introduction to Rules (Grammar ke do roop)

English grammar mein words ko badalne (jaise plural banana, past tense banana) ke do tareeqe hote hain: Productive aur Non-Productive.

  • 🔹 Productive Rules: Yeh "zinda" rules hain. Agar kal koi naya word banta hai, toh yahi rule lagega.
  • 🔹 Non-Productive Rules: Yeh "itihas" (history) hain. Yeh sirf kuch purane chune hue words par lagte hain, naye words par nahi.

3. Productive Rules (Zinda Rules)

Yeh rules grammar ke default rules hain. Jab hume samajh nahi aata, hum yehi lagate hain.

  • Rule: Adding '-s' for Plural.
    Logic: Jab 2010 mein "Selfie" word aaya, toh uska plural "Selfies" bana. Humne 'Selfien' ya 'Selfiece' nahi bola kyunki '-s' lagana ek productive rule hai. Ek naya brand "Google" aaya, toh "Googles" bolte hain.
  • Rule: Adding '-ed' for Past Tense.
    Logic: "I WhatsApped him." WhatsApp ek naya verb hai, past tense banane ke liye productive rule '-ed' lagaya.
  • Rule: Adding '-ness' for Noun.
    Logic: "Awesomeness". Awesome ke aage '-ness' lagakar noun bana diya.

4. Non-Productive Rules (Purane/Dead Rules)

Yeh rules pehle chala karte the (Old English mein), par ab naye words par apply nahi hote.

  • Rule: Vowel Mutation (Andar ka vowel badalna).
    Example: Man -> Men, Foot -> Feet, Sing -> Sang.
    Logic: Yeh purane words hain isliye inka vowel badla. Par agar naya word "Batman" (brand) ka plural banana ho, toh technically "Batmans" zyada logical lagta hai naye bachon ko, though formally it's Batmen. Par "Mouse" (animal) ka "Mice" hota hai, lekin "Computer Mouse" ka plural "Computer Mouses" bhi accept kiya jata hai kyunki naye meaning ke liye log productive rule ('-s') lagate hain!
  • Rule: Adding '-en' for Plural.
    Example: Ox -> Oxen, Child -> Children.
    Logic: Kya Box ka plural Boxen hoga? Nahi, "Boxes" hoga. Kyunki '-en' ab mar chuka hai (non-productive).

5. Top Mistakes Students Make

  1. Over-regularization: Bachche sochte hain sab past tense '-ed' se banenge.
    Wrong: I goed, I buyed.
    Right: I went, I bought. (Yeh non-productive forms yaad hi karne padte hain).
  2. Double Plurals: Non-productive se plural ban chuka hai, fir bhi '-s' lagana.
    Wrong: Childrens, Womens, Peoples.
    Right: Children, Women, People.
  3. Applying dead rules to new words:
    Wrong: I used two computer mice. (While technically correct historically, "mouses" is the standard productive adaptation for the device).

Story: The Tech Market Trip

Rohan wanted (Productive: -ed) to upgrade his home setup. He went (Non-productive: Go -> Went) to the electronics market. The market was full of men and women (Non-productive vowel mutation) buying gadgets.

First, he bought (Non-productive) two new computer mouses (Productive rule applied to a modern word). The shopkeeper tried to sell him old items, but Rohan Googled (Productive: -ed on brand name) the reviews quickly. He avoided the shopkeeper's cleverness (Productive: -ness).

While walking back, he saw some children (Non-productive: -en) playing with smartphones (Productive: -s). They were taking selfies (Productive: -s). Rohan smiled, realizing how fast technology changes (Productive: -s). He texted (Productive: -ed) his brother that he was heading home.
