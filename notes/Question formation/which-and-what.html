Which vs What: Asking the Right Questions

Category: Question Formation
Prerequisite: Interrogative Pronouns

  • Which: Used when there is a limited, specific, or known set of choices. (e.g., "Which of these two shirts do you like?")
  • What: Used when there is an unlimited or unknown set of choices. (e.g., "What is your favorite color?")

Nuance: Sometimes both work. "What train did you catch?" (general) vs "Which train did you catch?" (implying looking at a timetable).

Top Mistakes

  • Using 'what' when offering someone a direct choice (Wrong: "What hand is the coin in?" -> Right: "Which hand is the coin in?").

Story Context

Raju went to an ice cream parlor. The vendor asked, "What flavor do you want?" Raju looked at the 3 boxes left. "Which of these is eggless?" he asked. "What a great question!" the vendor smiled.
