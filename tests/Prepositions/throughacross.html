1. Prerequisites (Pehle ye samjhein)

Is topic ko deeply samajhne ke liye in concepts ka aana zaroori hai. Agar doubt hai toh pehle inhe padhein:

  • Prepositions of Movement - Words indicating motion from one place to another.
  • 2D Surface vs 3D Volume - Understanding flat spaces (lines/surfaces) vs enclosed spaces (volumes/containers).

2. 'Across' = 2D Surface (Ek side se dusri side)

Jab hum kisi flat surface ya line ko ek end se doosre end tak cross karte hain, tab hum 'Across' use karte hain. Isme depth ya boundary ka andar hona zaroori nahi hai.

  • Meaning 1: Movement from one side to the other (Movement).
  • Meaning 2: On the opposite side (Position/Location).

Ex 1: We walked across the road.

Reason: Road ek flat surface hai (2D). Humne ek kinare se dusre kinare tak travel kiya.

Ex 2: The bank is across the street.

Reason: Yahan position batayi ja rahi hai. "Sadak ke us paar".

3. 'Through' = 3D Space (Kisi cheez ke andar se guzarna)

Jab aap kisi 3-Dimensional space, volume, ya jhund (crowd/forest) ke andar se hote hue dusri taraf nikalte hain, toh 'Through' lagta hai. Isme aap surrounded (ghire hue) hote hain.

  • Meaning 1: Moving in at one end and out at the other (Tunnel, Forest, Water).
  • Meaning 2: By means of / Because of (Kisi ke zariye).

Ex 1: The train went through the tunnel.

Reason: Tunnel ek enclosed 3D space hai. Train uske andar gayi aur bahar nikli.

Ex 2: I got this job through a friend.

Reason: Abstract use. Friend ek medium (zariya) tha jiske dwara job mili.

4. Key Nuances & Top Mistakes

Mistake 1: Looking 'Across' vs 'Through' the window

Wrong: I looked across the window.

Right: I looked through the window.

Logic: Window ek frame/volume create karti hai (glass ke aar-paar dekhna). Aap surface ke upar nahi chal rahe, andar se dekh rahe hain.

Nuance: Walking in a Park

Walked across the park: Focus is on crossing it fast. Maano park ek flat maidan hai.

Walked through the park: Focus is on the environment. Ped (trees), ghaas, bench ke beech se hote hue guzre (3D volume).

Quick Test Rules:

  • Is it a flat line/surface? (Road, River, Border, Screen) -> ACROSS
  • Does it have top/bottom/sides? (Tunnel, Forest, Window, Doorway, Crowd) -> THROUGH

Contextual Story

Observe how 'through' and 'across' naturally alter the meaning based on 2D vs 3D spaces in this daily commute story.

It was a rainy Monday. Rajat had to reach his office, which was located right across the main city bridge. He grabbed his umbrella and started walking.

First, he had to navigate through the dense crowd at the local market. The market had tall stalls on both sides, making it feel like a narrow, busy tunnel. After escaping the crowd, he stood at the edge of the street. He looked both ways and carefully walked across the road to reach the bus stop.

When the bus arrived, he got in and stared through the foggy window. The bus drove across the long, flat bridge spanning the river. After getting off, Rajat walked through the revolving glass doors of his office building. He had made it, learning that life is just a journey of moving across obstacles and through challenges.
