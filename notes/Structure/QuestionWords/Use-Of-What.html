When to use "What"

1. Prerequisites (Pehle ye samjhein)

Is topic ko samajhne ke liye, aapko in concepts ki basic knowledge honi chahiye:

  • WH- Question Words (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How)
  • Auxiliary / Helping Verbs (is, am, are, do, does, did)

2. What vs. Which (Choice ka chakkar)

Hum "What" tab use karte hain jab options infinite (unlimited) hote hain. Aur "Which" tab use karte hain jab options finite (limited ya pehle se pata) hote hain.

Logical Example:

❌ Which is your name? (Galat, kyunki duniya mein infinite naam hain).

✅ What is your name? (Sahi).

✅ Which shirt do you want, the red one or the blue one? (Yahan options limited hain, toh Which aayega).

3. What vs. Who (Insaan ya Cheez?)

"What" generally non-living things, ideas, ya actions ke liye use hota hai. Insaanon (people) ke liye hum "Who" use karte hain. Nuance: "What" insaan ke liye tab use hota hai jab hum uska profession pooch rahe ho.

  • Identity: Who is that man? (Wo aadmi kaun hai?)
  • Profession: What is your father? (Aapke pitaji kya kaam karte hain?) - Note: "What does your father do" is more modern and polite.

4. What vs. How (Bada Confusion)

Ye Indian speakers ki sabse badi galti hoti hai. Agar aap kisi cheez ki appearance (dikhti kaisi hai) pooch rahe hain, toh "What... look like" use karein. Agar nature/health pooch rahe hain toh "How" use karein.

✅ What does the new office look like? (Office kaisa dikhta hai? Bada hai, chhota hai?)

✅ How is the new office? (Office kaisa hai? Mahol kaisa hai?)

❌ MISTAKE: How does the new office look like? (Ye sentence structure bilkul galat hai. 'How' ke sath 'like' nahi aata end mein).

5. What in Indirect Questions

Jab "What" sentence ke beech mein aata hai (as a conjunction/relative pronoun), toh uske turant baad helping verb (do/does/is) nahi aati, balki subject aata hai. Ye direct question nahi reh jata.

  • Direct Q: What do you want? (Tumhe kya chahiye?)
  • Indirect Q: I know what you want. (Mujhe pata hai tumhe kya chahiye.)
  • ❌ MISTAKE: I know what do you want. (Galat. Beech mein 'do' nahi aayega).

6. Top Mistakes Indians Make

  • Saying "What is your good name?" (Direct translation of 'shubh naam'. Just say "What is your name?").
  • Saying "How do you call this in English?" (Wrong. It should be "What do you call this in English?").
  • Saying "What for you are going?" (Wrong order. Should be "What are you going for?" or "Why are you going?").

A Day in the Life: Using 'What'

Observe how "What", "Which", and "How" are used in daily conversations.

Rahul woke up late and rushed to the kitchen. His mother asked, "What do you want for breakfast?" (Infinite choices). Rahul was in a hurry. He looked at the counter and asked, "Which is ready faster, the poha or the upma?" (Limited choices - only two items).

On his way to work, his bike broke down. He called a mechanic. The mechanic asked, "What happened to the engine?" Rahul sighed, "I don't know what is wrong with it." (Notice: not 'what is wrong' but acting as a statement clause here. Wait, actually "I don't know what is wrong" is correct because 'what' is the subject of the clause. But if it was object: "I don't know what it is.").

At the office, Rahul's boss introduced a new client. Rahul whispered to his colleague, "Who is that?" (Insaan ke liye Who). Later, he had to file a report. His colleague asked, "What does the new template look like?" (Not 'How does it look like'). Rahul replied, "I can show you what it looks like." (Indirect question - subject 'it' comes before verb 'looks').
